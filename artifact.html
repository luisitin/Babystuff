How to spot a fake freebie

Real baby freebies are common, and so are copycats. These habits keep you safe.

Stay on the brand's own site

Real programs live on the brand's own site. Check the address bar before typing anything.

No card for a free sample

A free sample never needs your card. A shipping fee should be labeled up front on a well-known retailer's site.

Be wary of "free trials"

"Free trials" that take a card usually become subscriptions. Read the renewal terms and set a cancel reminder.

Skip survey "free baby box" offers

Surveys promising a "free baby box" or gift card for your phone number are lead-gen sites that sell your details.

Red flags in the page

Watch for look-alike domains, countdown timers, "only 3 left!" and "share to unlock".

Never share SSN or bank logins

Never give a freebie site your SSN or bank login. For an insurance breast pump, use a supplier your insurer lists.

Don't tap links in ads or texts

Don't tap freebie links in ads or texts. Go to the brand's site yourself.

Use a dedicated email

Use a dedicated email or a plus address (name+baby@gmail.com) to see who shares your address.

Report scams

Report scams at ReportFraud.ftc.gov. Type it into your browser yourself.
